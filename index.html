01

About

My PhD in Computer Science at the University of Florida, advised by Kristy Boyer and Bonnie Dorr, developed generative agent-based models of learners and calibrated them to population-level behavioral targets through test-time compute allocation.

My current work trains models against those behavioral targets directly with reinforcement learning. Before and during the PhD, I worked on task-oriented dialogue and synthetic data generation at Amazon Alexa, Openstream.ai, and Rasa.

02

Research program

A simulated population is useful as a scientific instrument only if its aggregate behavior matches what real populations do. My work treats that match as the objective: I measure where LLM simulations of people diverge from human behavior, calibrate them at inference time, and train models so that the calibration becomes a property of the model.

A · Fidelity

Belief-behavior consistency

Role-playing agents can describe how a persona will behave and then simulate it doing something else. In LLM simulations of human trust, models' stated predictions about a persona do not reliably match the behavior they generate for it, so a simulation's self-report cannot substitute for behavioral validation.

COLM 2025
B · Calibration

Calibration as test-time compute allocation

My dissertation holds the generator and the target specification fixed and asks how inference-time compute should be distributed across a simulated population. Across fifty behavioral targets for simulated pair programming, whether a target is defined per session or over the whole batch predicts both its difficulty and which allocation strategy works.

Dissertation, 2026
C · Authoring

Simulating learners from composable hypotheses

HYP-MIX lets education researchers specify learner behavior as combinations of testable hypotheses and checks whether LLM-simulated learners stay behaviorally calibrated when those hypotheses are modified. It is the framework the learner-simulation work in my dissertation builds on.

AAAI 2025Code
D · Training

Training against behavioral targets

Search at inference time can make a population hit its targets, but it pays that cost on every run. My current work moves the objective into training and uses reinforcement learning to optimize models directly against population-level behavioral targets.
