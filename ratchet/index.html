The ADHD tax

Willpower isn't the problem. Visibility is.

Most productivity apps assume you'll notice when you've drifted and remember where you were. Those are the two things an ADHD brain is worst at. Ratchet takes both jobs off your plate.

Time blindness

An hour vanishes into a “quick” tab. You only find out at the end of the day, when it's too late to do anything about it.

a clock that counts every minute, and a score that moves as you work or drift.

Starting cold

A big goal with no obvious first move just sits there, making you feel worse every time you look at it.

break any goal into a tiny next step and start it in one tap. The timer is already running.

The lost thread

Switch tasks once and the context is gone: which tabs, which notes, what you were about to try.

each goal keeps its own workspace and a Breadcrumb that says where you left off.

The part nobody talks about

You stopped believing your own “I'll do it today.”

After enough broken plans, the hardest part of ADHD isn't the work. It's that you no longer trust the person who promised to do it.

Pep talks don't fix that, and neither do bigger plans. What rebuilds trust is evidence: small promises, kept, and seen.

So Ratchet shrinks the promise to one sitting (“by the end of this, I will have…”), asks honestly at the end whether you did, and keeps the record. A “not yet” is just a not yet: it's parked with a note, never held against you. Over a week the record starts to say something your inner critic can't argue with.

How it works

One loop, every sitting.

Ratchet lives in your menu bar and runs one simple loop. It's short enough to keep doing on a bad day, which is the only day that matters.

  1. Say what, and why

    Every sitting starts with a task and the goal it serves. Work with no goal can't start at all: the bad path is impossible, not just discouraged.

    By the end of this sitting, I will have…

  2. Every minute counts

    The clock runs from the moment you start. Time nobody accounted for shows up on the day as it happens, not at midnight.

    18 minutes in · on track

  3. Still on it?

    Every 20 minutes, a single tap. Say yes and carry on. Say you drifted and start again, no lecture. Silence counts as drift.

    Still on it? · Yes · I drifted

  4. Did you finish it?

    Each sitting ends with an honest answer before anything new can start. Finished, not yet, or parked for later with a note.

    Did you push the fix? · Yes · Not yet

Why it works

Built on what the research supports.

Ratchet borrows from a few of the better-replicated findings in behaviour change. It isn't a treatment. It's scaffolding.

Plans in “when, then” form

Deciding exactly what you'll do before a sitting starts makes you more likely to follow through. Every Ratchet sitting opens with that decision.

Implementation intentions: Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006, meta-analysis of 94 studies.

Watching your progress

People who track progress toward a goal are more likely to reach it, especially when they record it and can see it. The day score and the timeline do exactly that.

Progress monitoring: Harkin et al., 2016, Psychological Bulletin.

Make the bad path impossible

Designs that rely on willpower fail on the days it's needed most. So Ratchet won't start work without a goal, and won't start something new until the last thing has an answer.

Design principle: change the defaults, not the person.

Straight talk

What Ratchet is, and isn't.

It is

  • A firm, friendly structure for the hours you want to spend on purpose.
  • Honest: a fixed daily bar that never adapts to make a bad day look good.
  • Kind about drift: one tap and you're back. No streaks to lose and no guilt trips.
  • Yours: your own data, synced through your own cloud.

It isn't

  • A medical treatment for ADHD, or a replacement for one.
  • Surveillance: it never records your screen or your keystrokes. It only looks at what's open when you ask it to set up a workspace.
  • Another to-do list that fills up and then gets ignored.
  • For Windows, yet. It's Mac first, plus a web app on your phone.

Questions

FAQ

Won't a check-in every 20 minutes break my focus?

It's one tap, and it never takes over your screen. If you're deep in the work, “Yes, still on it” takes under a second. If you've drifted, that tap is exactly the interruption you needed.

What happens when I ignore it?

An unanswered check-in counts as drifted. That's on purpose: with ADHD, drifting is usually silent, so silence is treated as a sign of it. You can always correct a sitting afterwards.

Do I need to plan my whole day first?

No. You only ever name the next sitting. Goals and deadlines help when you have them, but “Break it down” turns anything into a five-minute first step.

Where does my data live?

Ratchet syncs through a small cloud service that runs in your own Cloudflare account; the installer sets it up. Nothing is sold, and the public dashboard only shows what you choose to share.

Does it work without the AI coach?

Yes. Torque, the coach, is optional and runs on your own Claude subscription. Everything else works without it.

What does it cost?

The download is free. A Pro plan for the coach, Workspaces and the extras is planned; what's free today stays free.
